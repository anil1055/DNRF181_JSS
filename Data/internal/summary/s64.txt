To create a comprehensive summary of the research paper "Software Cost Estimation Models Using Radial Basis Function Neural Networks" by Ali Idri, Azeddine Zahi, Emilia Mendes, and Abdelali Zakrani, let's break down the content into sections and highlight the key findings.

**Introduction**
-----------------

The authors introduce the importance of accurately estimating software development effort, citing the potential consequences of incorrect estimates. They emphasize that current methods include parametric and non-parametric approaches, focusing on non-parametric models specifically involving Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs).

They present Radial Basis Function Neural Networks (RBFNNs) as a suitable approach for approximating any continuous function, making them useful for software cost estimation.

**Background on RBFNs**
-------------------------

Moody and Darken introduced the concept of Radial Basis Functions (RBFs) inspired by biological receptive fields. RBFNs consist of three layers: an input layer, a middle layer containing hidden nodes (radial basis functions), and an output layer. The middle layer processes input data through local receptive fields (Gaussian functions), allowing for efficient mapping between inputs and outputs.

For software cost estimation, RBFNs use historical project data to learn relationships between input factors (predictors) and outcomes (response variable – effort). The process involves determining the locations and widths of the receptive fields within the input space, ensuring good coverage without redundant overlap.

**Data Preparation**
-------------------

Two datasets are used: COCOMO'81 (252 projects) and the Tukutuku database (53 web applications). Both sets involve multiple predictor variables (attributes) influencing the response variable (project effort).

Attributes for COCOMO'81:
1. SIZE (Software Size)
2. DATA (Database Size)
3. TIME (Execution Time Constraint)
4. STOR (Main Storage Constraint)
5. VIRT_MIN (Virtual Machine Volatility)
6. VIRT_MAJ (Virtual Machine Volatility)
7. TURN (Computer Turnaround)
8. ACAP (Analyst Capability)
9. AEXP (Applications Experience)
10. PCAP (Programmer Capability)
11. VEXP (Virtual Machine Experience)
12. LEXP (Programming Language Experience)
13. SCED (Required Development Time)

Attributes for the Tukutuku database:
1. TeamExp (Average team member expertise in web development)
2. Devteam (Development team size)
3. WebPages (Total web pages in the application)
4. TextPages (Total text pages in the application)
5. Img (Total image count in the application)
6. Anim (Animation presence)
7. AudioVideo (Presence of audio/video elements)
8. TotHigh (Count of high-effort features)
9. TotLow (Count of low-effort features)

**Methodology**
--------------

The researchers employ two clustering techniques to initialize the positions of the radial basis functions (henceforth called 'nodes') in the middle layer of the RBFNN: 
- APCTriad III (APC-III)
- C-means algorithm

Their purpose is to partition the input data into groups of similar projects, creating representative centroids for each group. Two variations of each clustering algorithm produce distinct node placements, leading to differences in RBFNN behavior.

**Experiments**
---------------

Using a combination of machine-learning tools and manual evaluation, they analyzed various configurations of RBFNNs trained on the above datasets. Accuracy metrics included Mean Magnitude of Relative Errors (MMRE) and Prediction Level (Pred).

Results showed that:
- For the COCOMO'81 dataset, using C-means resulted in generally improved predictions over APC-III.
- On the Tukutuku dataset, the results indicated C-means produced slightly better accuracy.

Key finding: The effectiveness of both algorithms varies significantly depending upon the complexity of the specific dataset being processed.

**Conclusion**
----------

Given the varying complexities observed in datasets, recommendations derived from the study propose employing the C-means clustering algorithm for developing cost estimation models utilizing RBFNs.
