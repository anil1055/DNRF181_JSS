The primary focus of this paper is on assessing whether adaptation rules can enhance web cost estimation accuracy. To evaluate this, researchers replicated a previous study conducted by Mendes et al. (2001) and extended their findings by incorporating Feature Subset Selection (FSS) as an additional component.

### Key Findings and Used Predictors

From the original study by Mendes et al. (2001):

1.  **Dataset**: There were two datasets analyzed: Dataset 1 consisted of 12 projects from a single company, while Dataset 2 comprised 37 projects from multiple companies.
2.  **Methodology**: Researchers used the jackknife method, leaving out individual projects iteratively and evaluating various combination sets (size measures, adaptation rules, and case adaptation techniques) to predict effort accurately.
3.  **Adaptation Rules**: Two adaptation strategies were implemented:
    *   **Linear size adjustment**, accounting for variations in size-related metrics to refine predicted efforts.
    *   **Subjective weighting**, assigning custom weights to certain attributes during calculations.
4.  **Effectiveness of Adaptation**: Initially, results revealed that using adaptation rules didn't provide enhanced predictive performance on the messy Dataset 2. However, when focusing on Dataset 1, which contained fewer irregularities, adaptation techniques demonstrated notable improvements in predicting costs.

Now, let us look at how adding Feature Subset Selection impacted the outcomes:

*   With FSS incorporated:
    *   Using FSS helped to generally enhance prediction precision in both datasets.
    *   Although there wasn't always perfect alignment with non-adaptive versions, suggesting potential assistance in the adaptation process, especially concerning the choice of predictor attributes.
    *   For DS2, having lesser instances of optimal predictions after introducing FSS might imply a detrimental impact of excessive variability on the usefulness of specific adaptive processes utilized.

Key effort estimation predictors listed include:

*   Team Experience
*   Website Pages Developed From Scratch (New WP)
*   Text Typed (TextTypied)
*   Images Created/New (ImgNew)
