## Abstract:

The paper discusses the introduction of Object-Oriented Hypermedia Function Points (OO-HFP), a functional size measurement procedure for Web projects developed using the Object-Oriented Hypermedia (OO-H) method. This method uses a model-driven and transformation-based approach to estimate the size of a Web application based on its conceptual model. The authors propose the usage of Case-Based Reasoning (CBR) for estimating the effort involved in completing a Web project.

## Methodology Overview:

The authors discuss the importance of model-driven architecture (MDA) for Web development due to its ability to transform a conceptual model into executable code. They argue that conventional software metrics are insufficient for measuring the complexity of modern Web applications, so there is a need for novel size measurement tools like OO-HFP. The methodology involves creating a conceptual model of the Web application, assigning sizes to individual components, and calculating the overall size of the application. Three essential stages are outlined in the measurement process: designing the measurement method, applying the measurement method, and exploiting the measurement results.

## The OO-H Development Process:

The paper explains the Object-Oriented Hypermedia (OO-H) method, which serves as the foundation for the OO-HFP metric. OO-H emphasizes the use of visual representations (models) to express a website's characteristics and behaviors. The developers utilize models to guide the creation of a Web application, ensuring consistency throughout the development process. Key concepts, such as Requirement Models, Platform-Independent Models (PIMs), and Platform-Specific Models (PSMs), form the core of the OO-H framework.

## Design of OO-HFP:

In designing the OO-HFP metric, the researchers adhere to the general process model for software measurement proposed by Jacquet and Abran. Four critical tasks are mentioned in the initial step of the process: specifying the objective, characterizing the concept under consideration, selecting the metamodel, and establishing numerical assignment regulations. OO-HFP focuses on accurately assessing the functional size of a Web site by leveraging the principles of the IFPUG method. By doing so, the OO-HFP ensures comparability with numerous projects documented in the Industry Benchmark Service Group (ISBSG) database.

## Automation of OO-HFP in VisualWADE:

VisualWADE is used to implement OO-HFP. The process begins with documenting the conceptual model using the OO-H method. Next, the OO-HFP mapping guidelines are employed to generate the FPA representation of the functional size for the conceptual model. The plugin applies the OO-HFP measurement criteria and FPA rating standards to compute the functional size of the Web application. Users receive immediate feedback regarding both the quantity of points allocated to various capabilities and the program's overall size in function points.

## Validating OO-HFP for Effort Estimation:

For comparison reasons, the Tukutuku size measures are utilized alongside the OO-HFP for evaluating prediction precision. These Web size metrics, founded on actual project data collected via surveys and interviews, have been recognized as reliable indicators of the resources necessary to finish a project efficiently. Utilizing the Commercial Tool CBR-Works, the Case Based reasoning algorithm predicted the success of the project. Predictions demonstrated limited accuracy when utilizing either OO-HFP or Tukutuku size measures alone for making projections. Nevertheless, neither performed worse than baselines computed from averages or media efforts of previous projects.

## Conclusion:

The article proposes a strategy for automatically computing the size of web programs based on conceptual designs, offering potential advantages in the areas of productivity assessment and task scheduling. Despite the preliminary outcomes demonstrating modest correlation between the projection precision achieved with OO-HFP/Tukutuku size measures and those attained with historical project numbers, this proof-of-concept demonstrates the usability of a size metric grounded on contemporary MDA ideas.

Predictors Used in the Research:

1. **Functional Size**: Measured as the sum of the product of the volume factor (Vf) and the difficulty factor (Dt). Vf depends on the functional component, and Dt depends on the complexity factors such as cyclomatic complexity, Halstead complexity, etc.

2. **Number of Web Pages (NP)**: This refers to the total number of web pages in the system.

3. **Number of Images (NI)**: The total number of images displayed in the system.

4. **Features (NF)**: Total number of distinct business functionalities offered by the system.

   - Features of low effort (LF):
     o Text boxes
     o Drop-down menus
     o Radio buttons
     o Checkboxes

   - Features of medium effort (MF):
     o Buttons
     o Links

   - Features of high effort (HHF):
     o Complex queries/search engines
     o Online payment gateway
     o User login/profile management

5. **Reusable features (RF)**: The percentage of reusable features, categorized into RF-High and RF-Low, indicating whether they require significant modifications before deployment.

6. **Total Weighted Factors (TWPF)**: Calculated by multiplying the weightage of each feature by the number of occurrences and adding them together.

The efficiency of the OO-HFP was evaluated relative to the Tukutuku size measures by employing case-based reasoning (CBR) and statistical methods. The findings indicate that both measures exhibited comparative effectiveness, although none surpassed historic averages as benchmarks. Overall, the study signifies the viability of using contemporary model-driven architectural strategies for efficient measurement of complex system dimensions.
